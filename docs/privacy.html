Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026

The short version

Steady doesn't collect, send or share any of your information. There's no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no server. Everything you put in Steady stays on your phone.

The only time anything goes online is if you choose to shout us a coffee in the Google Play version. Google Play's own checkout handles that payment.

What Steady stores, and where

Steady saves what you enter only in its own private storage on your phone. That includes your tasks, reminders, mood check-ins, journal entries, voice recordings, photos, files, projects, rewards and step counts. Nobody else can see it, including the person who made Steady.

If you uninstall Steady, Android deletes all of it. If you make a backup, Steady saves a file wherever you choose, such as your phone's storage or Google Drive. What happens to that file after that is up to you.

What Steady doesn't do

  • It doesn't ask you to sign up or log in.
  • It doesn't send your information anywhere. The version from GitHub has no internet permission at all. The Google Play version has it only because Google's checkout needs it for the optional coffee.
  • It has no ads, analytics or crash reporting, and no third-party code that collects data.
  • It doesn't sell, share or rent your information, because it never has it.

Shouting us a coffee

Everything in Steady is free. In the Google Play version you can choose to shout us a coffee from Settings. It's optional and doesn't unlock anything.

Google Play handles the whole payment. Your card or payment details go to Google, never to Steady or to me. Like every app sold on Google Play, I get Google's usual record of each order, such as the order number, the amount and the date. Steady itself only remembers, on your phone, how many coffees you've shouted, so it can say thanks.

In the GitHub version, if there's a coffee button, it opens a tip page in your web browser. That page has its own privacy policy.

Phone permissions, and why

Steady only asks when you use the feature that needs it, and you can say no or turn any of them off later in your phone's settings.

NotificationsTo remind you about your tasks.
Alarms & remindersSo reminders arrive on time rather than a few minutes late.
CameraOnly when you choose to add a photo. The photo is saved inside Steady.
MicrophoneOnly when you record yourself in "Work through it". Recordings stay inside Steady.
Physical activityOnly if you turn on step counting. Steady reads your phone's step counter and keeps the totals on your phone.
Photos and filesOnly the ones you pick yourself, to attach to a task or project or to restore a backup. Steady keeps its own copy.
Run at start-upSo your reminders come back after your phone restarts.
Google Play checkout and internet (Google Play version only)Only for the optional coffee. Google's checkout needs them. Steady never uses them to send your information.

Links and phone numbers

When you tap a support line or a link, Steady hands it to your phone app, messages app or web browser. Those apps and websites have their own privacy policies. Steady doesn't know what happens after that.

Downloads

If you downloaded Steady from GitHub, GitHub counts how many times the file was downloaded, the same as for any file. If you got it from Google Play, Google provides the usual store statistics to the developer, such as install numbers by country and Android version. Neither tells me who you are, and Steady itself never reports anything.

Steady is not medical care

Steady is a self-help tool for habits and wellbeing. It isn't a medical device and it doesn't diagnose or treat anything. If you're struggling, please talk to your GP or a counsellor. If you're in crisis, call your local emergency number or a crisis line. In Australia that's Lifeline on 13 11 14. Elsewhere, see findahelpline.com.

Children

Steady isn't made for children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, at any age.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. Steady's code is open, so anyone can check what it does: github.com/jaidoonser/steady.
